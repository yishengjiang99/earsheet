How to turn audio into sheet music

Three steps, about five seconds to your first notes.

STEP 1

Play, sing or hum

Piano, guitar, voice or anything with a melody. Use the microphone live, or import a recording in the app.

STEP 2

Watch the notes appear

Notes land on the staff as you play, chords included. Tempo, time signature and key are worked out for you.

STEP 3

Play it back and export

Hear it back with each note lit up, check it on the piano roll, then export PDF, MIDI or MusicXML for MuseScore, Logic or GarageBand.

Questions

How do I convert audio to sheet music?

Open the free demo, press record and play the music (or import a file in the iPhone app). Notes appear on a staff as it listens. When you stop, you get a score with tempo, meter and key, which you can export as PDF, MIDI or MusicXML.

Is there a piano transcription app that works live?

Yes. AI Music Radar transcribes piano as you play, including chords, and shows the notes on a two-staff score and a piano roll with playback. It also works for guitar, voice and other melodic instruments.

Can I hum a tune and get sheet music?

Yes. Hum, sing or whistle into the microphone and AI Music Radar writes the melody as notes on a staff, so you can catch a tune before you forget it.

Can I try it without installing anything?

Yes. The web demo runs in your browser with no sign-up. The iPhone and iPad app is coming soon to the App Store; join the waitlist to hear when it launches.

Is my audio uploaded?

No. In the iPhone app transcription runs on your device and recordings are never uploaded. It works in airplane mode.

What can I export?

PDF sheet music, MIDI and MusicXML (for MuseScore, Dorico, Sibelius, Finale, Logic or GarageBand), plus MP3 and a PNG photo of the score.

What does the free plan include?

Free: 3 saved pieces and 30-second clips, with live transcription and MP3 and photo export. Pro ($4.99/month or $29.99/year, 7-day free trial) or Lifetime ($49.99 once) removes the limits and adds MIDI, MusicXML and full-length PDF export.

How accurate is the transcription?

Clear recordings of one instrument work best. Notes, chords and rhythm come out well enough to read and edit; dense mixes with drums and many instruments are harder. Try the demo with your own playing to see.

Technical details, for the curious

Audio is captured at 22 kHz mono and run through Spotify's open Basic Pitch model (Core ML, on the Neural Engine) in 2-second windows. A Swift decoder turns the model output into notes, and a quantizer finds tempo, meter and key and snaps notes to a 16th-note grid before engraving.

  • Note accuracy (F1) on public benchmarks after decoder tuning: GuitarSet 84.3 (was 80.2), MAESTRO piano 73.5 (was 69.5), SMD piano 76.2 (was 70.3).
  • Note loudness (velocity) error after calibration: 9.2 (was 19.4).
  • Web demo latency is about 0.7 s.
